## Motion Principles Specification — Rounded Rectangle, Horizontal Travel with One Full Rotation

**Status:** Shared, application-neutral creative specification. No application was opened, rendered, or tested; every application-specific mapping below is deferred to `application-adaptation`.

---

### Objective
A single rounded rectangle traverses the frame horizontally, left to right, while rotating exactly one full revolution, so that the two motions read as one deliberate, legible gesture. The outcome must be achievable — and comparably good — in Alight Motion and Adobe After Effects, judged on the rendered result rather than on matching internal controls.

### User requirements (verbatim intent, not modified)
1. The subject is a **rounded rectangle**.
2. It **travels horizontally across the frame**.
3. It **rotates exactly once** (one full revolution).
4. It must **work equally well in Alight Motion and After Effects**.

No other parameters were supplied. Nothing below reclassifies a user requirement as a recommendation.

---

### Open choices for the user
These decisions materially change the observable result and are therefore presented rather than silently chosen. My recommended default is given in brackets.

| # | Decision | Options | Recommended default |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | Travel extent | (a) fully off-frame at both ends, (b) starts off-frame left, ends fully inside the right edge, (c) spans edge to edge, half-off at both ends | **(a)** — reads as a complete traverse |
| C2 | Rotation direction | (a) clockwise, (b) counter-clockwise | **(a) clockwise** |
| C3 | Easing character | (a) linear (constant rate, mechanical), (b) ease-in-out (depart/settle), (c) snappy ease-out with a brief hold | **(b) ease-in-out**, applied identically to travel and rotation |
| C4 | Canvas format | (a) 16:9 landscape, (b) 9:16 vertical, (c) 1:1 square | **(a) 16:9 landscape** |

### Subject and role
- A single rounded rectangle is the sole animated subject; no other layers, text, or decor are implied.
- Role: the moving subject that carries both the horizontal trajectory and the rotational gesture. Because it is the only element, it is simultaneously the focus and the motion's only readable subject.
- **Symmetry caveat (observability):** a rounded rectangle has 180° rotational symmetry, so a single full revolution can be misread as a half turn, and a still frame cannot distinguish 360° from 0°. *Optional proposal:* a single contrasting accent (e.g. one corner or edge in a second colour) makes "exactly once" legible to a human reviewer. This is optional and adds an element the user did not request; the numeric criterion in the acceptance criteria stands regardless.

---

### Spatial path
- Direction: **strictly horizontal**, left → right. No vertical drift, no arc, no path curvature. Vertical position is constant at the composition's vertical centre.
- Extent (per C1 default (a)): the shape starts **completely outside the left edge** and ends **completely outside the right edge**. Its centre travels a horizontal distance of **one composition width plus one shape width** — i.e. it clears the frame by half its own width on each side.
- Distance is expressed **relative to the composition** so it holds for any canvas: displacement = W + w, where W = composition width and w = shape width. This avoids inventing an absolute pixel distance that would not survive a canvas change.
- No overshoot, no rebound, no pause at the end. The subject is off-screen for the whole tail of the shot under C1(a).
- The reference point for position is the **shape's own centre (anchor/pivot)**, not a corner and not the frame centre. This is stated so that the two applications place the same point on the same pixel.

### Rotation / scale / opacity
- **Rotation:** exactly one full revolution, i.e. a net change of **360°** (or −360° if counter-clockwise is chosen). Start 0°, end 360°. The path is **monotonic** — no reversal, no back-and-forth, no overshoot past 360°.
- **Pivot:** the shape rotates **about its own geometric centre**. Rotating about any other origin would turn the motion into an orbit and break the brief.
- **Scale:** constant at 100% throughout. No scaling, no pulsing.
- **Opacity:** constant at 100% throughout. No fade in or out; entry and exit are accomplished purely by travelling off-frame (per C1 default). A fade would conflict with "travels across the frame" being self-evidently a traversal.
- **Corner radius, fill, and any stroke:** constant throughout. The fill colour and any stroke are unspecified by the brief and are treated as an **unknown** (see Dependencies).

**Proposed defaults (single labelled line):** duration 3.0 s · 30 fps · canvas 1920×1080 landscape · travel C1(a) off-frame to off-frame · rotation clockwise · shape width = 20% of composition width · shape aspect 2:1 (w:h) · corner radius = 20% of the shorter side, clamped to ≤ 50% of the shorter side.

**Frame-rate note (not a request for clarification):** the spec is authored in seconds so it is frame-rate independent; at 30 fps one revolution over 3.0 s averages 4°/frame, and at 25 fps it averages 4.8°/frame. Both are below the perceptual threshold for stepping. The frame rate only matters for (a) the exact frame count of the end keyframe and (b) whether each application's timebase matches. If the two applications are built at different frame rates, both must be set to the same rate for the durations to match.

### Easing character
- **Recommended character (C3 default):** *soft ease-in-out* on both travel and rotation — the subject accelerates gently off the left edge, holds a near-constant middle speed, and decelerates into the right edge. It should read as "deliberate", not "springy" and not "mechanical".
- **Alternative (a):** *linear* — constant rate throughout, reads as a mechanical/technical motion (e.g. a gear or a conveyor part).
- **Alternative (c):** *snappy ease-out* — quick departure, long glide, with an optional brief hold at the end; useful if the shot continues past the subject's exit.
- Constraints on any choice:
  - The **same easing character must be applied to travel and rotation**. If the two differ, the rotation will appear to accelerate or decelerate relative to the travel; that can look intentional, but it must be a deliberate, stated choice rather than an accident of per-property defaults.
  - No overshoot or anticipation anywhere — overshoot would add a second partial revolution at the end and violate "exactly once".
  - Described by observable character, not by a named control or a curve value. The two applications do not share an easing vocabulary, and their equivalent controls will differ in name, availability, and range; the mapping is a task for `application-adaptation`. **Not verified whether either application exposes arbitrary/free-form curve shaping; treat as unknown.**
- If the applications cannot express a matched easing character, the fallback is to keep the easing simple (linear or a single ease-in-out) and match **easing character** rather than an identical curve — parity is judged on the rendered gesture, not on identical controls.

---

### Composition and hierarchy
- **Canvas:** aspect ratio unknown. Proposal: 16:9 landscape at 1920×1080; a 9:16 portrait variant is equally valid and changes only the pixel dimensions and shape scale, not the motion logic (all distances are relative).
- **Hierarchy:** the rectangle is the only element and is therefore the sole focal point. No safe-area or legibility constraint applies to text because no text is in scope.
- **Shape proportions:** proposal — width = 20% of composition width, aspect 2:1 (w:h), corner radius = 20% of the shorter side. Keeping the corner radius as a proportion of the shape's own size (not an absolute pixel value) preserves the look on any canvas.
- **Colour:** not specified. Unknown. Proposal: a single flat fill with no gradient; a gradient would make the rotation read less clearly, since a rotationally symmetric shape with an asymmetric gradient can read as a wobble rather than a turn.
- **Framing:** the subject is intentionally absent from the frame for a moment at the start (or, under C1(b)/(c), partially present). No additional entrance treatment is implied.

### Transitions
- No cuts, wipes, or scene changes are specified.
- Entry and exit are **continuous off-frame travel**, not transitions in the edit sense.
- Nothing else enters, exits, or transforms during the shot.
- If a longer piece is later cut around this shot, the off-frame entry/exit are the natural handles; nothing in this spec depends on unseen adjacent material.

### Verification required in native application
The following must be checked in Alight Motion and in After Effects separately, and the results compared. No claim of parity is made here; parity is a target that only a native check can confirm.

1. **Rotation endpoint** — read the orientation value at the final keyframe in each app and confirm it is 360° ± 1° (or −360°) and monotonic in between. This is the single criterion most likely to fail silently.
2. **Rotation pivot** — confirm the turn is about the shape's own centre and that the recorded position reference is the same point in both apps.
3. **Direction** — confirm both apps resolve the same rotation direction, and the same left-to-right travel.
4. **Timing** — confirm both apps are set to the same frame rate, and that the start and end keyframes land on the same frame indices.
5. **Easing character** — compare rendered frames across the departure, middle, and arrival segments; the two should read alike even though the underlying controls will differ.
6. **Frame coverage** — confirm the subject is fully outside the frame at both ends in both apps.
7. **Delivery** — confirm both results export at the same resolution and duration for side-by-side review.

### Hand-off

Open questions to resolve there: rotation-direction and unit conventions; how a centre-anchored position is expressed and recorded; which easing characters are available in the target version; whether an arbitrary curve is possible; and what each app's native export/duration behaviour is. Neither plan may assume the other application's representation applies. I have not asserted how either application will interpret any of the parameters above.
