**What you asked for, in your words:** "Make my logo reveal feel more premium."

I can't see your project, a preview, or a reference right now, so I'm not going to name specific flaws in what you already have — I'd just be guessing. "Premium" also isn't one thing; it usually comes from one or two of a handful of causes, and the fix differs a lot depending on which one is actually carrying the weight.

**Review areas I'd work through once I can see it** *(my recommendations, not your brief):*

- **Hierarchy** — is there one clear moment of arrival, or are several elements competing for the same beat? Premium reveals almost always have a single focal beat and everything else supports it.
- **Typography** — letter-spacing, weight against the mark, and the settle of the wordmark. Tight, uneven, or crowded type reads cheap faster than any motion does.
- **Timing** — total duration and where the *hold* is. Reveals that never stop moving feel busy; the final rest is usually where the perceived quality comes from.
- **Easing character** — uniform or linear-feeling motion reads as default. Deceleration into the final position is the most common single upgrade.
- **Transitions / continuity** — whether elements arrive in sequence with overlap, or pop in independently.
- **Color and contrast / finish** — background separation, and any light, sheen, grain, or depth treatment. Restraint here usually helps more than adding more.

**Smallest useful input from you — any one of these gets me started:**

1. A short screen recording of the reveal at real speed, from first motion through the final frame, **or** 3–4 stills: first frame, midway, just before settle, final lockup.
2. Canvas size, frame rate, and total duration.
3. Whether the logo is a mark, a wordmark, or both, and whether it's vector or raster.
4. Any brand constraints you already have: typeface, colors, tone references.

**Two things only you can decide, because they change the whole direction:**

- **Restraint or spectacle?** A slow, minimal reveal with one precise settle, versus a more energetic build with multiple stages. Both can read premium; they're different products.
- **Where should the premium signal live** — in the *motion* (timing, easing, stillness), or in the *material* (light, metal, glass, grain, shadow)? If it's mostly material, that's largely a look-development conversation rather than a timing one.

If duration is flexible, tell me roughly how long the reveal is allowed to be.

Send the recording or stills and I'll come back with a concrete written spec — per-property timing, easing described by feel rather than by one app's controls, composition and hierarchy rules, and observable pass/fail criteria you can check against a preview. It'll be app-neutral so it works wherever you're building it.
