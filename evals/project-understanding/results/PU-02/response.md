**In the meantime, the changes that most often separate amateur from professional work.** These are proposals based on common patterns, not observations about your edit:

- **Timing and easing.** Constant-speed motion is the single most common tell. Nearly everything should ease in and out; nothing should stop dead at full speed. Key poses usually need to hold longer than feels natural at first.
- **Hierarchy.** One focal point per moment. Simultaneous motion on multiple elements reads as busy rather than energetic. Contrast — size, brightness, or color — should make the subject unmistakably dominant.
- **Typography.** Two typefaces at most, a consistent scale relationship, no stretched or squashed text, generous and *consistent* margins. Centering every element flattens hierarchy; left-aligned blocks usually read more considered.
- **Color.** A restrained palette, consistent grade across all shots so cuts don't jump in brightness or temperature, avoidance of default saturated primaries and pure black/white on dark backgrounds.
- **Transitions.** Fewer. Cut on motion where possible. The stock spin, zoom, and page-peel transitions are the fastest ways to look templated — straight cuts and match cuts look more deliberate.
- **Audio.** Even levels, a sound accent at cuts and key reveals. Poor audio reads as amateur faster than weak visuals do.
- **Finishing.** Consistent frame rate throughout, no upscaled footage, matched grain and vignette, and an end card with clean alignment.
